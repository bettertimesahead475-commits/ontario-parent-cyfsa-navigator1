# Visits with your child in CAS care: access orders

*Legal information, not legal advice. For advice about access in your case, contact a lawyer or Legal Aid Ontario at
1-800-668-8258.*

In the CYFSA, visits and other contact are called "access." Section 104 lets the court make, change or end orders about
access, and says who can ask.

## What the court can do (s. 104(1))

The court "may, in the child's best interests," when making an order under the child protection part of the Act or on
an application, "make, vary or terminate an order respecting a person's access to the child or the child's access to a
person, and may impose such terms and conditions on the order as the court considers appropriate."

Three things follow from that wording:

- **The test is the child's best interests.**
- **Access runs both ways:** a person's access to the child, and the child's access to a person.
- **Terms and conditions can be attached,** for example supervision, location or frequency.

## Who can apply (s. 104(2))

Where a child is in a society's care and custody or under its supervision, these may apply "at any time":

1. the child;
2. any other person, "including a sibling of the child" and, for a First Nations, Inuk or Métis child, a
   representative chosen by each of the child's bands and communities;
3. the society.

## Notice (s. 104(3) and (4))

A person applying under paragraph 2 must give notice to the society. A society making or receiving an application must
give notice to the child (subject to s. 79(4) and (5)), the child's parent, the person caring for the child, and, for a
First Nations, Inuk or Métis child, a representative chosen by each of the child's bands and communities.

## Practical steps

- Keep a simple log of every visit: date, length, who supervised, what went well, any concern raised and how it was
  resolved. Attach any written visit notes you receive.
- If visits are cancelled, note who cancelled and why.
- If you want more or different access, ask your lawyer whether an application under s. 104(2) is appropriate.
- Ask relatives, including siblings, whether they want contact; the Act lets them apply.
