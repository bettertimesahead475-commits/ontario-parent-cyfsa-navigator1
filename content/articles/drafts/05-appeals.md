# Appealing a child protection order in Ontario

*Legal information, not legal advice. Appeals have strict deadlines that are not set out in this section of the Act. If
you are thinking about an appeal, contact a lawyer immediately or call Legal Aid Ontario at 1-800-668-8258.*

Section 121 of the CYFSA sets out who can appeal an order made in a child protection case, which court hears it, and
what happens to the child's care while it is pending.

## Who can appeal (s. 121(1))

An appeal from a court's order under the child protection part of the Act may be made by:

- (a) the child, if entitled to participate in the proceeding under s. 79(6);
- (b) "any parent of the child";
- (c) "the person who had charge of the child immediately before intervention";
- (d) a Director or local director; or
- (e) for a First Nations, Inuk or Métis child, any of those people or a representative chosen by each of the child's
  bands and communities.

There is an exception: s. 121(1) "does not apply to an order for an assessment under section 98" (s. 121(2)).

## Which court hears it (s. 121(2.1))

- If the order was made at the **Ontario Court of Justice**, the appeal goes to the **Superior Court of Justice**.
- If it was made at the **Family Court of the Superior Court of Justice**, the appeal goes to the **Divisional Court**.

The appeal is heard "in the county or district in which the order appealed from was made" (s. 121(7)).

## What happens to the child's care during an appeal (s. 121(3) and (4))

When a decision about care and custody is appealed, "execution of the decision shall be stayed for the 10 days
immediately following service of the notice of appeal on the court that made the decision." If the child is in the
society's care and custody when the decision is made, the child "shall remain in the care and custody of the society"
until the 10-day stay expires or a temporary order is made, whichever is earlier. The appeal court may make
a temporary order for the child's care and custody, in the child's best interests, until the appeal is decided.

## Other points in the section

- **New evidence:** the appeal court "may receive further evidence relating to events after the appealed decision"
  (s. 121(6)).
- **Adoption:** "No extension of the time for an appeal shall be granted where the child has been placed for adoption"
  (s. 121(5)).

## Practical steps

- Get a copy of the order and the court's reasons as soon as possible.
- Contact a lawyer right away about the deadline and the forms; they are set by court rules, not by this section.
- Keep records of events after the decision; the appeal court may receive evidence about them.
