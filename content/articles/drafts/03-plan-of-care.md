# The society's plan of care: what section 100 requires

*Legal information, not legal advice. Talk to a lawyer about your case, or call Legal Aid Ontario at 1-800-668-8258.*

If a children's aid society asks the court for a supervision order, society care, or certain later orders, it has to
put its plan in writing. Section 100 of the CYFSA says what that plan must contain. For parents, the plan is one of the
most useful documents in the case: it states what the society says needs to change, and how it will decide its
involvement can end.

## When the plan is required

Section 100 says the court "shall, before making an order under section 101, 102, 114 or 116, obtain and consider a
plan for the child's care prepared in writing by the society." Section 101 covers the main protection orders
(supervision, interim society care, extended society care).

## What the plan must include

| Clause | The plan must include | Questions a parent can ask |
|---|---|---|
| 100(a) | "a description of the services to be provided to remedy the condition or situation on the basis of which the child was found to be in need of protection" | Which services, from whom, when do they start, and how do they match the concern? |
| 100(b) | "a statement of the criteria by which the society will determine when its care or supervision is no longer required" | What exactly must be shown, and how will it be measured? |
| 100(c) | "an estimate of the time required to achieve the purpose of the society's intervention" | Is the time estimate realistic given wait lists for the services named? |
| 100(d)(i) | where the child is or will be removed, "an explanation of why the child cannot be adequately protected while in the person's care, and a description of any past efforts to do so" | What past efforts are described, and are they accurate? |
| 100(d)(ii) | "a statement of what efforts, if any, are planned to maintain the child's contact with the person" | What contact is planned, how often, and where? |
| 100(e) | for permanent removal, "a description of the arrangements made or being made for the child's long-term stable placement" | |
| 100(f) | "a description of the arrangements made or being made to recognize the importance of the child's culture and to preserve the child's heritage, traditions and cultural identity" | What is planned for the child's culture, language and community? |

## Using the plan

- Ask for the plan in writing, and keep every version; plans can change over time.
- Compare the services in clause (a) with the concern the court found. If a service does not relate to the concern,
  ask why it is there.
- Track the criteria in clause (b). Keep dated records of each step you complete: attendance letters, certificates,
  test results, reports.
- If a plan leaves out a required element, raise it with your lawyer.
