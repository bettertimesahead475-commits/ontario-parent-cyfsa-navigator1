# CAS took my child: what must happen within five days

*Legal information, not legal advice. If your child has been taken, contact a lawyer now or call Legal Aid Ontario at
1-800-668-8258.*

When a child protection worker removes a child from home, the Act calls it bringing the child "to a place of safety."
Parents are often told little about what happens next. The CYFSA sets a firm outer limit: section 88.

## How a child can be brought to a place of safety (s. 81)

Section 81 gives three routes:

- **With a warrant (s. 81(2)).** A justice of the peace may issue a warrant if satisfied, on a worker's sworn
  information, that there are reasonable and probable grounds to believe the child is younger than 16, is in need of
  protection, and "a less restrictive course of action is not available or will not protect the child adequately."
- **By court order (s. 81(4)).** On a person's application, the court can order the child produced before the court,
  or brought to a place of safety.
- **Without a warrant (s. 81(7)).** A worker who believes on reasonable and probable grounds that the child is in need
  of protection, is younger than 16, and that "there would be a substantial risk to the child's health or safety during
  the time necessary" to get to a hearing or obtain a warrant, "may without a warrant bring the child to a place of
  safety."

The section also allows police assistance (s. 81(8)), entry to premises, by force if necessary (s. 81(6) and (10)), and
a worker to authorize a medical examination where a parent's consent would otherwise be required (s. 81(9)).

## The five-day limit (s. 88)

Section 88 says that "as soon as practicable, but in any event within five days after a child is brought to a place of
safety under section 81," one of these must happen:

- (a) the matter is brought before a court for a child protection hearing under s. 90(1);
- (b) the child is returned to the person who last had charge of the child (or to the person entitled to custody under
  an order enforceable in Ontario);
- (c) for a child under an extra-provincial order, the child is returned as that order requires;
- (d) a temporary care agreement is made under s. 75(1); or
- (e) an agreement is made under s. 77 (16 and 17 year olds).

Two points parents often miss:

- **"As soon as practicable" comes first.** Five days is the maximum, not the target.
- **Bringing the matter before the court is not the final decision.** Section 90(1) requires the court to hold a hearing
  to determine whether the child is in need of protection. The first appearance is often the start of the court
  process.

## What you can prepare in those days

- Write down, while you remember, what happened when the child was removed: date, time, who was present, what was
  said, and any document you were shown.
- Gather the child's essentials and records the court may ask about: school, medical, and any prior contact with the
  society.
- List people who could care for the child, or supervise visits, if the court considers that.
- Ask the worker in writing when and where the matter will be in court.
- Contact a lawyer or Legal Aid Ontario (1-800-668-8258) before the first court date.
