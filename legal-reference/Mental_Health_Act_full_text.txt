KEY DEFINITIONS

"Board" means the Consent and Capacity Board continued under the Health Care Consent Act, 1996.

"involuntary patient" means a person who is detained in a psychiatric facility under a
certificate of involuntary admission, a certificate of renewal or a certificate of continuation.

"rights adviser" means a person, or a member of a category of persons, qualified to perform
the functions of a rights adviser under this Act and designated by a psychiatric facility, the
Minister or by the regulations to perform those functions, but does not include a person
involved in the direct clinical care of the person to whom the rights advice is to be given.

PART II - HOSPITALIZATION

Application for psychiatric assessment (s. 15) - "Form 1"
A physician who examines a person may make an application for a psychiatric assessment where
the person has threatened/attempted self-harm, behaved violently toward another, or shown a
lack of competence to care for themselves, AND the physician is of the opinion the person is
suffering from a mental disorder likely to result in serious bodily harm to themselves,
serious bodily harm to another, or serious physical impairment of themselves. The application
is authority, for seven days from signing, to take the person into custody and detain them for
examination for up to 72 hours (s. 15(5)).

Duty of attending physician / conditions for involuntary admission (s. 20) - "Form 3"
After the 72-hour assessment period, the attending physician must either release the person,
admit them as an informal/voluntary patient, or - only if specific conditions are met -
complete a certificate of involuntary admission. Two separate sets of conditions exist:
s. 20(1.1) covers a person with a documented history of a recurring mental disorder who has
shown clinical improvement before but is now at risk again; s. 20(5) covers the more general
case: the physician must be of the opinion BOTH that the patient's mental disorder is likely
to result in serious bodily harm to themselves, serious bodily harm to another, or serious
physical impairment, AND that the patient is not suitable for admission as an informal or
voluntary patient.

Detention periods (s. 20(4)) - the actual timeline
- Certificate of involuntary admission: up to 2 weeks
- First certificate of renewal: up to 1 additional month
- Second certificate of renewal: up to 2 additional months
- Third certificate of renewal: up to 3 additional months
- Certificate of continuation (first or subsequent): up to 3 additional months each

Notice and rights advice (s. 38) - the real, actionable right
Whenever a certificate of involuntary admission, renewal, or continuation is completed, the
attending physician MUST (1) promptly give the patient a written notice, and (2) promptly
notify a rights adviser. The written notice must tell the patient: (a) the reasons for the
detention; (b) that they are entitled to a hearing before the Board; (c) that they have the
right to retain and instruct counsel without delay; and (d) where applicable, the right to
request specific Board orders under s. 41.1. The rights adviser must then promptly meet with
the patient in person and explain the significance of the certificate and their right to have
it reviewed (s. 38(3)). This applies even before formal certification: s. 38.1 requires the
same kind of written notice (reasons for detention + right to counsel) for a person merely
subject to a s. 15 application or s. 32 order, before any certificate is even issued.

Review by the Board (ss. 39, 41) - the real challenge mechanism
An involuntary patient, or anyone on their behalf, can apply to the Consent and Capacity Board
"to inquire into whether or not the prerequisites set out in this Act for admission or
continuation as an involuntary patient are met" (s. 39(1)). This can be done every time a new
certificate takes effect (s. 39(2)), and is automatically deemed to happen on the first
certificate of continuation and every fourth one after that even if the patient does nothing
(s. 39(4)) - a built-in safeguard against being forgotten in the system. At the hearing, the
Board must review whether the legal prerequisites are STILL met at the time of the hearing,
not just when the certificate was first signed (s. 41(1)), and must rescind the certificate if
they are not (s. 41(3)).

Appeal (s. 48)
A party to a Board proceeding may appeal the Board's decision to the Superior Court of Justice
on a question of law or fact or both.

Community Treatment Orders (s. 33.1 onward)
A separate, less restrictive mechanism: a physician may issue a community treatment order
requiring someone to comply with a treatment plan while living in the community rather than
being detained, but ONLY with the person's (or substitute decision-maker's) actual consent
(s. 33.1(4)(f)) - and specifically requires that the person has first consulted with a rights
adviser and been advised of their legal rights (s. 33.1(4)(e)), with narrow exceptions. The
person also has an explicit right to retain and instruct counsel and to be informed of that
right (s. 33.1(8)). A community treatment order can be challenged the same way - an
application to the Board under s. 39.1.
