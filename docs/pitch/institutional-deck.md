## Slide 2 — The Problem

**Headline:** One Document. Nine Different Checks.

**Left column: bullets (max 4)**
- A single CAS affidavit can run dozens of pages and has to be read several times over.
- Hearsay, unsourced statements and missing firsthand knowledge are easy to miss under a deadline.
- Contradictions *across* affidavits, notes and orders sit in different files.
- Review quality depends on who has time that week.

**Right column: mock "Evidence Profile" card**

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│ EVIDENCE PROFILE · Affidavit (Doc 1) │
│                                      │
│   48          45          7          │
│  flagged    evidence   contra-       │
│  items      gaps       dictions      │
│  (navy)     (amber)    (purple)      │
│                                      │
│ Evidence Strength Index  ▓▓▓░░░  Low │
│ Illustrative example · synthetic doc │
└──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**⚠ VERIFY (numbers):** As written, 45 gaps + 7 contradictions = 52, which is more than the 48 total. A lawyer in the room will notice. Do one of the following:
(a) Pull all three numbers from a real run on a **synthetic or fully de-identified** affidavit so they reconcile, or
(b) Label the metrics so they don't read as parts of one total, e.g. "48 statements reviewed · 45 lacking a named source · 7 cross-document conflicts".

The caption "Illustrative example · synthetic document" is required. Never show numbers from a real family's file.

**⚠ VERIFY ("Nine"):** Name all nine checks in your notes (or in an appendix slide) and make sure the product actually runs each one. If you can't list nine, change the number in the headline.

**Speaker notes:**
> "Every affidavit gets asked the same questions: who said this, did they see it firsthand, is it corroborated, does it match the other documents, which statutory ground is it being used for, and so on. Nine questions per document, repeated across a whole file. That's where the hours go, and it's where things get missed. The figures on the right come from a synthetic sample document, not a real case."

## Slide 3 — The Solution

**Headline:** How It Works

**Top band: 3 steps, left to right with thin blue arrows**

| ① Upload | ② Analyze | ③ Review |
|---|---|---|
| Affidavits, plans of care, notes, prior orders | Each statement is checked and sourced | Your staff review every flag, each linked to the passage it came from |

**Bottom band: 2×2 feature grid**

| **Evidence Strength Index** | **Issue Identification** |
|---|---|
| Scores corroboration, source reliability, consistency and completeness. It scores the evidence, not the side. | Separates documented fact, reported information, inference, allegation and legal conclusion. |
| **Cross-Document Analysis** | **CYFSA s.74 Mapping** |
| Sourced timeline across documents. A conflict is flagged only when two specific documents disagree. | Links allegations to the s.74(2) ground they appear to rely on, for counsel to assess. |

**Footer (Muted, 11 pt):** Analysis support only. Not legal advice. Findings are for review by counsel or qualified staff.

**Speaker notes:**
> "The design principle is that every output points back to the page it came from. The scoring is evidence-neutral by design: a document doesn't score higher because it helps the parent, or lower because it helps the Society. That matters to you, because anything you rely on has to hold up in front of a judge. On s.74 mapping, the tool shows which ground an allegation appears aimed at. Whether the ground is made out is a legal question for counsel."

## Slide 4 — Why It Matters

**Headline:** What Changes for Your Team

Four equal cards in a row. Each has a green check icon in a circle, a bold one-word title and one line of text.

| ✓ **Speed** | ✓ **Consistency** | ✓ **Documentation** | ✓ **Preparation** |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-pass review in minutes, not hours. | The same checks run on every document, every time. | Every flag links to its source passage. | Walk into case conferences with the questions already mapped. |

> **Why "Consistency" and "Preparation" instead of "Accuracy" and "Confidence":** An AI tool can't honestly promise accuracy on a slide, and "confidence" invites the question "confidence in what?" Consistency and preparation are claims you can prove in the demo. Keep "Accuracy" only if you have a documented validation study (e.g., agreement rate against lawyer review on N documents) that you can cite on the slide.

**⚠ VERIFY:** "Minutes, not hours" needs a real timing from your own test runs. State the page count when you use it, e.g. "~4 minutes for a 30-page affidavit".

**Speaker notes:**
> "The value isn't that the tool is smarter than your staff. It does the mechanical part the same way every time, so your people spend their hours on judgment: what to challenge, what to concede, what to bring to the community."

> **Be ready for these questions:**

## Appendix slide (recommended, not counted in the 6): "Where This Fits"

For band representatives, add one line showing you understand their legal position:
- Under CYFSA s.79(1), a representative chosen by the child's band or First Nations, Inuit or Métis community is a **party** to the proceeding.
- Some communities now act under their own laws through *An Act respecting First Nations, Inuit and Métis children, youth and families* (S.C. 2019, c. 24), which the Supreme Court of Canada upheld in 2024 (2024 SCC 5).

**⚠ VERIFY:** If the tool only maps to CYFSA, say so plainly: "Maps to CYFSA. Does not yet analyze community-specific laws." That gap is an honest roadmap item, and saying it will earn more trust than leaving it out.
